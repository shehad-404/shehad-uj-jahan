Research overview

I am a research-oriented Computer Science graduate and Research Assistant at the Institute of Water and Flood Management (IWFM), Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology. I develop data-driven forecasting models for renewable energy and environmental applications, and I am first author of an IEEE STI 2025 conference paper. I am an active contributor to “Six-Hour Shield”, a National Geographic Society-funded flash flood nowcasting project. My interest is in using advanced AI on spatiotemporal and climate-related data to address sustainability and resilience challenges. Alongside research, I am a detail-oriented full-stack web developer experienced in RESTful APIs, authentication systems, Docker and modern Git workflows, with a focus on clean code, performance and user-centric design. I am comfortable working in Linux (Ubuntu) environments, from administering an HPC cluster to scripting in Bash, and I am keen to work on Linux-based systems.

BSc Thesis Researcher

Department of CSE, Jagannath University · 2024 – 2025 · Dhaka, Bangladesh

Thesis: Optimized Autoformer for Long-Term Forecasting of Solar Irradiance and Wind Speed

  • Built a transformer-based deep learning framework for long-term forecasting of renewable energy parameters.
  • Forecast GHI, DHI, DNI and wind speed from multivariate time-series data.
  • Implemented and optimised the Autoformer in PyTorch, with preprocessing, feature engineering, sequence modelling and hyperparameter tuning.
  • Evaluated with MSE, RMSE and MAE. Findings published at IEEE STI 2025.

Research Computing & HPC

  • Configured and operated a multi-node Linux HPC cluster with head and worker nodes, SSH, networking and 10 GbE interconnects.
  • Used SLURM and MPI for distributed and parallel workloads.
  • Ran Delft3D simulations on the cluster using SLURM and MPI.
